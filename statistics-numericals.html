1. Arithmetic Mean — Step by Step

Mean = Σx / n
Frequency mean = Σfx / Σf
Numerical: Find mean of 10, 12, 15, 18, 20.

Step 1: Σx = 10+12+15+18+20 = 75.
Step 2: n = 5.
Step 3: Mean = 75/5 = 15.

Frequency numerical: x: 10,20,30 and f: 2,3,5.

fx: 20,60,150. Σfx=230 and Σf=10. Mean = 230/10 = 23.

Weighted mean: Values 60,70,80 with weights 2,3,5.

Σwx = 120+210+400=730; Σw=10; weighted mean = 73.

2. Median and Mode

Median numerical: Data = 7, 3, 10, 5, 9.

Arrange: 3,5,7,9,10. n=5, middle position=(5+1)/2=3. Median=7.

Even-number median: Data = 4,8,10,12.

Middle values are 8 and 10. Median=(8+10)/2=9.

Mode numerical: 2,4,4,5,6,4,7,5. The most frequent value is 4, so mode=4.
Grouped mode formula: Mode = l + [(f₁−f₀)/(2f₁−f₀−f₂)]h. Identify the modal class first, then substitute lower boundary, modal frequency, previous frequency, next frequency and class width.

3. Dispersion — Full Numerical

Data: 2, 4, 6, 8.

Mean = (2+4+6+8)/4 = 5.

Deviations: −3, −1, 1, 3.

Absolute deviations: 3,1,1,3; sum=8. Mean deviation about mean = 8/4 = 2.

Squared deviations: 9,1,1,9; sum=20. Variance = 20/4 = 5. SD = √5 = 2.236.

Range = Largest − Smallest
CV = (SD / Mean) × 100
CV for the above data: CV = (2.236/5)×100 ≈ 44.72%.
Quartile deviation: If Q₁=20 and Q₃=40, QD=(40−20)/2=10; coefficient=(40−20)/(40+20)=0.333.

4. Assumed Mean / Step Deviation

x̄ = A + Σfd/Σf
For equal class width: x̄ = A + h(Σfu/Σf)
Example: Take values 10,20,30 with frequencies 2,3,5 and A=20. d = −10,0,10. fd = −20,0,50. Σfd=30, Σf=10. Mean = 20 + 30/10 = 23.

5. Probability — Solved Numericals

Numerical 1: A fair die is thrown. Probability of getting an even number?

Sample space = {1,2,3,4,5,6}. Favourable = {2,4,6} = 3. P(E)=3/6=1/2.

Numerical 2: Probability of at least one head in two fair coin tosses.

Use complement. P(no head)=P(TT)=1/4. Therefore P(at least one head)=1−1/4=3/4.

P(A∪B)=P(A)+P(B)−P(A∩B)
P(A∩B)=P(A)P(B|A)

6. Binomial, Poisson and Normal — Numericals

Binomial: n=5, p=0.4. Find P(X=2).

q=0.6.
P(X=2)=⁵C₂(0.4)²(0.6)³
=10×0.16×0.216
=0.3456.

Mean=np=5×0.4=2. Variance=npq=5×0.4×0.6=1.2.

Poisson: λ=3, find P(X=2).

P(X=2)=e⁻³×3²/2! = 9e⁻³/2 ≈ 0.2240.

Mean=3 and variance=3.

Normal: μ=60, σ=5, X=70.

Z=(X−μ)/σ=(70−60)/5=2. Then use the standard normal table/software to obtain the required area.

7. Correlation — Step-by-Step

Pearson correlation measures linear association and ranges from −1 to +1.

Small example: X=1,2,3 and Y=2,4,6. Both variables rise perfectly linearly, Y=2X. Therefore Pearson r=+1.
r = Cov(X,Y)/(σxσy)
Interpretation: r close to +1 means strong positive linear association; close to −1 means strong negative linear association; near 0 means little linear association. Correlation alone does not prove causation.

8. Hypothesis Testing — Exam Method

Standard steps:
1. Write H₀ and H₁.
2. Select significance level α.
3. Select test statistic.
4. Calculate the statistic.
5. Find critical value or p-value.
6. Reject or fail to reject H₀.
7. Write conclusion in words.
One-sample mean z-style example: Suppose μ₀=50, sample mean=52, population σ=4, n=16. Then z=(52−50)/(4/√16)=2/(1)=2. Compare with the appropriate critical value for the chosen significance level and tail.

For a small sample with unknown population standard deviation, a t-test is often used under its assumptions.

9. Regression — Basic Numerical

Regression line of Y on X: Y = a + bX
Example: Suppose the fitted equation is Y = 5 + 2X. If X=7, predicted Y = 5 + 2(7) = 19.

In a real numerical, calculate slope/intercept using the required formulas or software, then substitute the given X to obtain the prediction.

10. ANOVA — What to Calculate

ANOVA compares means of three or more groups by separating variation into between-group and within-group components.

F = MS_between / MS_within
Exam flow: write H₀: all group means are equal → calculate sums of squares → calculate degrees of freedom → obtain mean squares → F ratio → compare with critical F or p-value → conclude.
