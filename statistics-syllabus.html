Module I — Introduction to Statistics

Definition & Scope: Statistics deals with collection, classification, presentation, analysis and interpretation of data. It is used in business, science, economics, research and computing.
Types of Data: Qualitative/quantitative, discrete/continuous, primary/secondary data.
Data Collection: Observation, interview, questionnaire, experiments and published sources.
Presentation: Bar diagrams, pie charts, histograms, frequency polygons and ogives.
Frequency Distribution: Organize observations into class intervals with frequencies; calculate class width, midpoint and cumulative frequency.

Module II — Measures of Central Tendency

Arithmetic Mean:
Mean = Σx/n ; Frequency mean = Σfx/Σf
Geometric Mean: GM = (x₁x₂...xₙ)^(1/n).
Harmonic Mean: HM = n / Σ(1/x).
Median: Ordered middle value; for grouped data use median-class formula.
Mode: Most frequent value. Grouped formula: Mode = l + [(f1−f0)/(2f1−f0−f2)]h.
Merits & Demerits: Mean uses all observations but is sensitive to outliers; median is robust but ignores distances; mode is simple but may be non-unique.

Practice: For 4, 6, 8, 8, 10, 12 find mean, median and mode.

Module III — Measures of Dispersion

Range: Maximum − Minimum.
Mean Absolute Deviation: Σ|x−A|/n.
Quartile Deviation: (Q3−Q1)/2.
Variance:
σ² = Σ(x−x̄)²/n
Standard Deviation: σ = √σ². Coefficient of variation = (σ/mean)×100.
Covariance: Measures joint variation of two variables; positive means they tend to move together.

Module IV — Probability & Distributions

Basic Probability: P(A)=favourable outcomes/total outcomes for equally likely cases. 0≤P(A)≤1.
Conditional Probability: P(A|B)=P(A∩B)/P(B).
Normal Distribution: Bell-shaped continuous distribution defined by mean μ and standard deviation σ.
Binomial: n independent trials, two outcomes, constant success probability p. P(X=x)=C(n,x)p^x(1−p)^(n−x).
Poisson: Rare-event count model. P(X=x)=e^(−λ)λ^x/x!.
R: R can calculate probabilities and plot distributions using functions such as dbinom, pbinom, dpois and ppois.

Module V — Correlation, Tests & Regression

Correlation: Pearson correlation r ranges from −1 to +1 and measures linear association.
T-test: Used to compare means when population variance is unknown, commonly for small samples.
ANOVA: Tests whether several group means differ significantly.
Simple Linear Regression:
y = a + bx
where b is slope and a is intercept.
Multiple Regression: Predicts a dependent variable from multiple predictors.
Generalized Linear Models: Extend linear models to non-normal response distributions using a link function.
